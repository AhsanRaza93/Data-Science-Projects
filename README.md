# Data-Science-Projects
This repository contains different Data Science Projects.

These projects are done as part of the assignments.

Here are names of all the projects with their descriptions.

1. Netflix Recommendation Engine:
Developed a movie recommendation system designed to provide personalized movie suggestions based on individual user interests and ratings. The project focuses on analyzing user preferences and movie ratings to identify popular genres and recommend the most suitable movies to users across different genres. It also involves evaluating genre-wise movie ratings to determine which genres receive the highest and lowest user ratings. The objective is to build a recommendation engine that improves the movie discovery experience by delivering suggestions tailored to individual preferences.

2. COVID-19 Data Analysis:
Performed exploratory data analysis on global and Indian COVID-19 datasets to understand the impact and progression of the pandemic. The project involves consolidating data from multiple CSV and Excel files using Pandas and analyzing trends in confirmed cases, deaths, and recoveries over time. Interactive visualizations were developed using Plotly to examine infection and recovery rates and understand the geographical and temporal distribution of the pandemic. Additionally, Facebook Prophet was used to develop time-series forecasting models to predict the expected number of COVID-19 cases for the following seven days based on existing trends.

3. Walmart Sales Analysis:
Conducted an in-depth exploratory and statistical analysis of Walmart's weekly sales data across multiple retail stores to identify sales patterns and understand the factors influencing business performance. The project involves data preprocessing, missing-value treatment, outlier analysis, and visualization to investigate the relationship between weekly sales and external factors such as unemployment rate, temperature, and Consumer Price Index (CPI). It also examines seasonal sales patterns, identifies the highest- and lowest-performing stores, and evaluates the differences in their historical performance. Furthermore, predictive modeling techniques are applied to forecast weekly sales for each store over the next 12 weeks, providing insights that can support inventory planning and demand management.

4. Customer Churn Prediction:
Analyzed customer data from a telecommunications company to identify customer behavior and understand the factors contributing to customer churn. The project involves data manipulation, filtering, statistical analysis, and visualization to explore customer demographics, service subscriptions, tenure, payment methods, contract types, and monthly charges. Various visualizations, including bar plots, histograms, scatter plots, and box plots, are used to identify patterns and relationships within the customer data.

Additionally, multiple machine learning algorithms are implemented to predict customer churn, including Simple Linear Regression, Simple and Multiple Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. The models are trained and evaluated using different train-test split ratios, confusion matrices, accuracy scores, and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), wherever applicable. The objective is to identify customers who are likely to discontinue their services and uncover useful insights that can help the company improve customer retention.

5. Image Classification:
Developed a deep learning-based image classification model to distinguish between two categories of images: cats and dogs. The project uses a dataset containing 1,000 images, equally distributed between the two categories, with 500 images of cats and 500 images of dogs. The objective is to train a model capable of learning distinguishing visual features from the image dataset and accurately classifying previously unseen images into their respective categories. This project explores the application of deep learning and image recognition techniques to solve a binary image classification problem.

6. Revenue Prediction:
Developed a machine learning regression model to predict restaurant revenue using various business-related attributes available in the dataset. The analysis considers factors such as restaurant name, franchise status, restaurant category, number of items offered, and total orders placed to understand their relationship with revenue generation. Data preprocessing, exploratory analysis, and feature selection are performed to identify relevant variables that influence restaurant revenue. Different machine learning approaches can then be applied to establish relationships between the independent variables and the target revenue variable. The objective is to estimate restaurant revenue and identify the business factors that contribute to revenue generation.

7. Analyzing Naming Trends:
Performed historical data analysis to explore baby naming patterns and identify popular names across different years. The project involves extracting datasets stored in compressed ZIP files using Python libraries such as Pandas, Zipfile, and BytesIO, followed by consolidating and processing the relevant data files. The analysis focuses on visualizing the number of male and female babies born in a particular year and examining the frequency and popularity of different baby names. Birth counts are aggregated and sorted to identify the most popular names, with the top 100 names further analyzed and grouped to uncover naming trends. The objective is to understand how naming preferences have evolved over time and identify popular names based on historical birth records.

8. Bio-Signal Analysis for Smoking:
Developed a machine learning classification model to identify the presence or absence of smoking in individuals using biomedical signal and health-related data. The project uses a dataset containing approximately 55,000 records and 27 attributes, representing different biological and physiological characteristics of individuals. The analysis involves examining the available features, understanding their relationships, and identifying relevant patterns that may be associated with smoking behavior. Machine learning techniques are applied to classify individuals into smokers and non-smokers based on their bio-signal information. The objective is to explore the application of data-driven classification techniques in identifying smoking-related patterns from physiological measurements.

9. Census Income Prediction:
Developed a machine learning classification model to predict whether an individual's annual income exceeds $50,000 based on demographic and socioeconomic information. The project begins with data preprocessing and exploratory data analysis to understand the dataset, examine feature distributions, identify relationships between variables, and uncover meaningful patterns. Various data visualizations are used to investigate the influence of attributes such as age, education, occupation, working hours, and other demographic characteristics on income levels. Multiple machine learning classification algorithms are then implemented and evaluated to predict the income category of individuals. The objective is to identify the key factors associated with income levels and build an effective classification model for income prediction.

10. Credit Score Classification:
Developed a machine learning classification system to categorize individuals into different credit score brackets based on their banking and credit-related information. The project utilizes a dataset containing approximately 100,000 records and 28 features, providing information about individuals' financial backgrounds and credit behavior. The analysis involves data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, and feature examination to understand the relationships between financial attributes and credit score categories. Machine learning classification algorithms are applied to identify patterns in the data and predict the appropriate credit score category for each individual. The objective is to automate credit score classification, reduce manual assessment efforts, and support more efficient credit risk evaluation.

11. COVID-19 Data Analysis:
Performed data-driven analysis of COVID-19 trends using global and Indian datasets containing information about confirmed cases, deaths, and recoveries. The project involves combining multiple CSV and Excel files using Pandas to create a consolidated dataset for further analysis. Interactive visualizations are developed using Plotly to examine the spread of infections, recovery rates, and changes in pandemic trends over time. Facebook Prophet is utilized to build time-series forecasting models to estimate the number of COVID-19 cases expected over the following seven days based on historical trends. The objective is to understand the progression of the pandemic through data visualization and statistical forecasting.

12. Credit Card Fraud Detection:
Developed a machine learning-based fraud detection system to identify potentially fraudulent credit card transactions and distinguish them from legitimate transactions. The project focuses on analyzing transaction data to understand the distribution of genuine and fraudulent activities and address the challenges associated with financial fraud detection. Data preprocessing and missing-value analysis are performed using Pandas, followed by calculating the number and percentage of fraudulent and legitimate transactions. Data visualizations are used to compare both transaction categories, while StandardScaler is applied to normalize transaction amount values and create a new normalized feature. Decision Tree and Random Forest classification algorithms are trained using a 70:30 train-test split. Their predictions, accuracy scores, and performance metrics are compared to evaluate their effectiveness in detecting fraudulent transactions. The objective is to identify the better-performing model for classifying suspicious credit card transactions.

13. Customer Churn:
Performed customer data analysis for a telecommunications company to investigate customer churn and identify patterns that may contribute to customers switching to competing service providers. The project involves extensive data manipulation and exploratory analysis, including customer segmentation based on demographic characteristics, tenure, payment methods, contract types, internet services, and monthly charges. Different visualization techniques, such as bar plots, histograms, scatter plots, and box plots, are used to understand customer distributions and relationships between important variables.

Multiple statistical and machine learning approaches are implemented to analyze customer behavior and predict churn. These include Linear Regression, Simple and Multiple Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. The models are trained using different train-test split ratios and evaluated using confusion matrices and accuracy scores. The objective is to understand the characteristics of customers who are likely to leave the company, evaluate different predictive models, and generate insights that can help improve customer retention strategies.

14. Flight Booking Price Prediction:
Performed exploratory data analysis and statistical investigation of a flight booking dataset containing approximately 300,000 records and 11 attributes to understand the factors influencing airline ticket prices. The project focuses on examining flight booking information, identifying pricing patterns, and exploring relationships between different flight-related features and ticket fares. Data analysis and visualization techniques are used to uncover meaningful insights from the dataset, while statistical methods help understand the distribution and behavior of flight prices. Machine learning algorithms are applied to develop a predictive model capable of estimating flight ticket prices based on the available attributes. The objective is to identify important factors affecting airfare and provide data-driven flight price estimates that can help passengers make informed booking decisions.

15. Heart Disease Prediction:
Developed and evaluated machine learning classification models to identify whether a patient is at risk of heart disease based on medical and clinical attributes. The project begins with exploratory data analysis, including descriptive statistical analysis, examination of feature distributions, and correlation analysis to understand relationships between different medical variables. Data visualizations are created to compare patients with and without heart disease, analyze the relationship between age and disease occurrence, and examine feature correlations through heatmaps.

Three classification algorithms—Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest—are implemented using a 70:30 train-test split. Their performance is evaluated using confusion matrices, classification reports, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The Decision Tree and Random Forest models are also visualized using the Graphviz package to understand their decision-making structures. Finally, the performance of all three classifiers is compared to identify the most suitable model for the heart disease classification task.
